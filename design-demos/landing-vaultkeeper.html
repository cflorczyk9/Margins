A few promises

What Margins
will and won't
do to your
files.

  1. i. Your sources never move. Originals live in raw_sources/. Margins only writes into the wiki/ folder next to them. Nothing is overwritten.
  2. ii. Margins asks before it writes. Every source page, every concept candidate, every entity update is shown to you first. Approve, edit, or reject — per file.
  3. iii. The AI is your AI. Bring a Gemini, OpenAI, or Anthropic key. Margins makes the calls. There is no key sync, no Margins server, no telemetry.
  4. iv. Open files, in any text editor, forever. Markdown bodies, YAML frontmatter, JSON metadata. Your vault is plain text — readable with Obsidian, vim, or a directory listing.
  5. v. The instructions ship with the vault. Every Margins vault includes an operator manual, a query cookbook, and command and agent files — so the next model that reads your vault knows how to behave.

How it works

A keeper, not an oracle.

Margins isn't a model. It's the layer underneath the model — the place where evidence is filed, named, and held still long enough to be useful. Three things keep it honest.

One · The shape

Two folders. One rule.

On your disk, Margins is two directories sitting beside each other. raw_sources/ holds the originals you brought — transcripts, PDFs, notes, exports. wiki/ holds the pages the AI writes from them: source pages, concept pages, entity pages, syntheses.

Margins never edits anything in raw_sources/. The evidence stays evidence. The wiki is where memory accrues.

raw_sources · wiki

Two · The arrangement

Bring your own AI.

Margins doesn't host a model and doesn't want to. Drop in a Gemini, OpenAI, or Anthropic key. The call goes straight from your machine to your provider.

No proxy. No key sync. No Margins servers in the path. The files stay on your disk — we wouldn't have anywhere to put them anyway.

No telemetry. No accounts. No login.

your key · your provider

Three · The handshake

The vault asks first.

When you drop in a source, Margins doesn't just file it. It shows you the source page it wants to write, the concepts it noticed, the entities it would update — laid out in front of you, before anything touches the wiki.

You approve, edit, or reject per file. The reviewed-first ingest is the whole point: a model writes drafts, a person decides what survives.

What Margins is for

The context engine,
not the chatbot.

Margins is the architecture: a vault, a way of ingesting, a way of asking. It is the same substrate I use under Briefly — the vertical layer for wealth advisors, where the wiki holds clients, families, and meetings instead of concepts and citations.

If you write for a living or think for a living and you want a place that remembers what you've read — across model upgrades, across vendor changes, across years — Margins is for you. If you run a firm and want that same shape pointed at your clients, Briefly is the version that's wearing a suit.

Same shape. Two registers. Both yours.
